I struggle asking for help, fearing I am wasting others’ time and that it may reflect poorly on me. Last year a pivotal moment came when I faced a dead end during my participation in the CAHSI Local Research Experiences for Undergraduates program. I was supervised by Professor Gabriel Elkaim from UCSC’s Autonomous Systems Lab on a research project, separate from any class or academic credit. It was a real-world task with professional stakes and impacting my self-image. Dr. Elkaim, an expert in robotics, wanted for his students a tool to make state machine diagrams for use during his mechatronics courses. My task was to develop that tool. Initially, I used regular expressions to identify patterns in the code. Despite initial progress, this method soon proved inadequate due to the sheer complexity and variability in coding structures like nested if-then-else and switch statements.

At this pivotal moment I faced a dead end and was reluctant to seek help. Back then ChatGPT was less known. Consulting it appealed to me because I did not worry about wasting ChatGPT’s time or what it would think about me. ChatGPT suggested that I try using a C parser and Abstract Syntax Trees (ASTs). With further assistance from ChatGPT, I found PycParser and taught myself XPATH and XSLT. Today the result is a tool that has received positive feedback from Dr. Elkaim and we are preparing an IEEE article about it. This experience with ChatGPT showed me the power of AI tools for researchers who, like in my situation, are stuck in dead ends and struggle asking for help. It led to my belief in AI’s potential to accelerate research.

My passion for technology in education was fueled when I tried for a month volunteering with East Palo Alto Tennis and Tutoring (EPATT) program, where I tutored math for children with less resources and opportunities. My own childhood learning experience with DragonBox Algebra, a game that made algebra both fun and intuitive, was a stark contrast to my experience with conventional tutoring. This realization emphasized the importance of teaching children using educational technologies that are fun.

I carried this idea and my experience with ChatGPT over to a summer project where my team and I used GPT3, StableDiffusion XL, Suno Bark and MusicGen to build a story generator able to entertain and educate children. It allows children to pick emoji to choose what the story is about and during the story they can choose what happens next in the plot so that they can experience the consequences of their decisions. Convincing my team to work with me on my idea was a challenge due to its ambitious nature. However, once I explained my motivations and experiences, the team agreed and the project’s success shows the potential of adding generative AI to educational software.

My work with Dr. Elkaim and ChatGPT, my volunteering experiences, and the success of my team’s AI-driven storytelling project have all shaped my aspiration to pursue a master’s degree in computer science. I am driven by a commitment to help use AI technology for educational equity, supporting children who have less resources and addressing the challenges faced by researchers like myself. I am excited to bring this perspective to UC Berkeley and contribute to an environment where technology enhances learning for everyone.
